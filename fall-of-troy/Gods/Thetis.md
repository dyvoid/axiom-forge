## Description
A sea-nymph of the Nereids, Thetis was fated to bear a son greater than his father — a prophecy that prompted Zeus and Poseidon to abandon their pursuit of her and marry her off to the mortal king Peleus instead. Her son was Achilles, the greatest warrior of his age, and her relationship with him defines her presence in the Iliad.

She was fully aware that Achilles faced a choice between a long, unremarkable life and a short one of eternal glory. He chose glory; she could not unmake that choice. So she did what she could: she dipped him in the Styx to protect him, she interceded with Zeus on his behalf when Agamemnon dishonoured him, and she commissioned Hephaestus to forge him new armour after Patroclus was killed wearing the old set.

Her grief runs beneath the entire poem — a mother who knows how the story ends, who cannot change it, and who loves her son anyway.
