# Trojan Horse

## Basic Information
- **Type:** Structure
- **Forged By:** Epeius, master carpenter, with Athena's guidance
- **Abilities:** Concealment of soldiers, Deception of an entire city, End of a ten-year siege

## Description & History
A wooden horse of extraordinary size, built by the Greek carpenter Epeius on the inspiration and with the divine guidance of Athena. Its construction took three days. It was hollow, with a trapdoor in its belly large enough to allow armed men to enter and exit, and it was built to appear as a votive offering — the kind of oversized religious gift that wealthy cities sometimes commissioned.

The horse was the material form of Odysseus's stratagem: a lie so large and so strange that the sheer audacity of it would make people believe. No one would build something that elaborate as a trick. No one would hollow out an enormous horse, fill it with soldiers, and leave it on a beach and sail away unless it was really an offering. The plan worked because it was preposterous enough to be taken seriously.

Inside the horse on the night Troy fell: Odysseus, Neoptolemus, Menelaus, Diomedes, and a company of the best Greek fighters. They waited in the dark while the Trojans celebrated around them, while Helen walked around the outside and called out the names of Greek warriors in the voices of their wives — perhaps testing, perhaps hoping. No one spoke. The horse stood still.

At midnight, Sinon gave the signal. The trapdoor opened.
