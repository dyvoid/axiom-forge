## Description & History
Troy burned in a single night. The Greeks who had hidden in the wooden horse emerged at midnight, killed the sentries, and opened the city gates. The fleet that had sailed to Tenedos returned under cover of darkness. By the time the Trojans understood what was happening, there was no longer a perimeter to defend.

The sack was total. The men were killed. Priam was murdered at the altar of Zeus by Neoptolemus — a deliberate sacrilege, the killing of an old man in a holy place, that offended even the gods who had wanted Troy destroyed. Women and children were taken as slaves. Hector's son Astyanax was thrown from the walls. Cassandra was dragged from Athena's temple.

A few escaped. Aeneas carried his father Anchises on his shoulders out of the burning city, leading his young son and a small company of Trojans. He took the Palladium with him and the sacred flame of the household gods. He would wander for years before reaching Italy, where his descendants would eventually found Rome — Troy's revenge, long and patient, dressed in the name of a new empire.

The Greeks sailed home. Most of them had terrible voyages. Agamemnon was murdered in his bath by his wife. Odysseus spent ten more years at sea before seeing Ithaca. The victors of the Trojan War did not have good endings.
