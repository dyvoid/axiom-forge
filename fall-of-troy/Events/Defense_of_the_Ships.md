# Defense of the Ships

## Basic Information
- **Type:** Battle
- **Date:** Year 10 of the Siege
- **Location:** [[Locations/Greek_Camp]]

## Description & History
With Achilles refusing to fight, the Trojans sensed an opportunity unlike any they had seen in ten years of war. Hector led the Trojan army across the plain, through the Greek defensive wall and ditch, and to the very ships on the beach. Greek commanders fell. The defensive line buckled. At one point Hector reached a ship and called for fire.

It was the closest the Trojans ever came to ending the war on their terms. Another hour and the Greek fleet burns; without ships, the army is stranded; stranded, they are slaughtered or enslaved on foreign soil.

It was also the moment that broke Patroclus. Watching from beside Achilles' tent, unable to stand the sight of Greeks dying while the greatest warrior in the world sat ten minutes away doing nothing, he went to Achilles and begged. Achilles would not go himself. He was not finished being angry. But he let Patroclus go in his armour, with his Myrmidons, to drive the Trojans back.

The ships did not burn. But the cost of saving them would be measured in the events that followed.
