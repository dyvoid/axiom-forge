## Description & History
Paris, a prince of Troy, visited Sparta as a guest of King Menelaus. Ancient hospitality laws — xenia — were among the most sacred obligations in the Greek world; to violate them was to invite divine punishment. Paris violated them.

Whether Helen left willingly or was taken by force remains the defining ambiguity of the entire tradition. Homer presents her in the Iliad with a complexity that resists simple answers: she is neither a helpless victim nor an enthusiastic accomplice, but a woman trapped between divine compulsion, the desires of powerful men, and her own impossible position. Aphrodite's influence over Paris — and perhaps over Helen — blurs any clean reading of consent.

What is unambiguous is the result. Menelaus returned to find his guest gone and his wife gone with him. He called on the oath that every former suitor of Helen had sworn — to defend her marriage by force if needed — and Agamemnon, his brother, turned that oath into an army. A thousand ships. Ten years.
