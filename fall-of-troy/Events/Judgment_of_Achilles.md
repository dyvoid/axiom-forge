# Judgment of Achilles

## Basic Information
- **Type:** Personal, Political
- **Date:** Year 10 of the Siege
- **Location:** [[Locations/Greek_Camp]]

## Description & History
In the tenth year of the war, the Greek camp was struck by plague sent by Apollo, punishing Agamemnon for enslaving Chryseis, a priest's daughter. A prophet confirmed the cause; Agamemnon agreed to return Chryseis — but announced he would take Achilles' captive Briseis as compensation for his loss.

This was not a quarrel over a woman. It was a public act of humiliation between the commander-in-chief and the most powerful warrior in the army. Achilles had received Briseis as his prize of honour from a shared campaign. To have her taken back stripped him of the recognition that was, in Homeric culture, the only real currency a warrior possessed. He had not fought for pay or land. He had fought for honour, and Agamemnon had publicly taken it from him.

Achilles withdrew from the fighting entirely. He asked his mother Thetis to petition Zeus to punish the Greeks for the insult by letting them lose until Agamemnon begged forgiveness. Zeus agreed. Greeks died in numbers for weeks. The Trojans pushed to the very ships. And Achilles stayed in his tent and played his lyre and waited.
