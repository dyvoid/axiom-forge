## Description & History
The idea was Odysseus's. After ten years and every military approach exhausted, he proposed a stratagem: build a giant wooden horse, hollow, large enough to conceal a company of the best Greek fighters. Leave it on the beach as an apparent offering to the gods. Sail the fleet away — visibly, so Trojan scouts would report it — and hide behind the nearby island of Tenedos. Leave one Greek behind, a man with a story to tell.

The Trojans came out of the walls for the first time in years to look at the abandoned camp and the enormous horse. A Greek named Sinon was found — planted, though he appeared captured — and told them the horse was a sacred offering to Athena, made oversized so the Trojans could not bring it inside the walls and gain the goddess's favour. Bringing it inside, he said, would make Troy impregnable. Leaving it outside or destroying it would bring divine punishment on the Greeks.

Cassandra warned them. The priest Laocoon warned them and was killed by serpents while he spoke. The Trojans voted. They opened the Scaean Gate, removed sections of the wall that the horse would not fit through otherwise, and dragged it inside. They celebrated with food and wine and went to sleep.

The Greeks came out of the horse at midnight. They opened the gates. The fleet had returned. By dawn, Troy was burning.
