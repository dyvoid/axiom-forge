## Description & History
Patroclus entered battle wearing Achilles' armour, and the Trojans fell back believing Achilles had returned. He drove them from the ships, killed Sarpedon — son of Zeus — and pushed all the way to the walls of Troy. Achilles had told him to go no further than the ships. Patroclus did not stop.

At the walls, Apollo intervened directly. He struck Patroclus from behind — knocked his helmet off, shattered his spear, stripped his shield. Dazed, unarmed, Patroclus was wounded first by the Trojan Euphorbus, then finished by Hector, who took Achilles' armour from the body as a prize.

The news came to Achilles at his tent. He collapsed to the ground and poured ash on his head. Thetis heard his cry from the depths of the sea and came to him. He told her he was going to kill Hector. She told him that meant his own death would follow. He said he did not care.

This moment — this exchange between a mother and her son who is choosing to die — is the emotional centre of the Iliad. Achilles' grief was absolute. His rage had been about honour; this was something older and less forgiving.
