## Description & History
A prophecy declared that Troy could not fall while the Palladium — a sacred statue of Athena kept in her temple within the city — remained inside the walls. Odysseus and Diomedes infiltrated Troy by night to steal it.

The accounts differ: in some versions Odysseus disguised himself as a beggar and entered through the gate, was recognised by Helen (who kept the secret), and located the statue. In others, they scaled the walls together under cover of darkness. In all versions, they succeeded — two Greeks walked into an enemy city, took a divine object from a temple, and walked out again.

The theft was both a practical military action and a statement: Troy's divine protection could be removed by human ingenuity. Athena, whose statue it was, had already chosen her side. The Palladium was later said to have been carried by Aeneas to Italy, where it became one of the sacred objects of Rome.
