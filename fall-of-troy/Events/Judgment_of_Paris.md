# Judgment of Paris

## Basic Information
- **Type:** Divine, Personal, Cosmic
- **Date:** Before the war — c. 1250 BCE
- **Location:** [[Locations/Mount_Ida]]

## Description & History
The event that set the Trojan War in motion. At the wedding of Peleus and Thetis — Achilles' parents — the goddess Eris, not invited, threw a golden apple into the gathering inscribed "for the fairest." Three goddesses claimed it: Hera, Athena, and Aphrodite. Zeus, unwilling to choose between them, delegated the judgment to Paris, then a shepherd on the slopes of Mount Ida, known for his sense of beauty.

Each goddess offered Paris a bribe. Hera offered dominion over kingdoms. Athena offered martial skill and wisdom without equal. Aphrodite offered the most beautiful woman in the world. Paris chose Aphrodite. The woman was Helen, wife of Menelaus of Sparta. The consequences of that choice would kill approximately everyone Paris had ever known.

The goddesses who lost — Hera and Athena — became implacable enemies of Troy. Aphrodite, who won, protected Paris and later Helen as a matter of divine honour. The divine factions in the war were not chosen arbitrarily; they were chosen at a shepherd's campfire on a mountainside, before the war existed, by a young man who wanted a beautiful woman and did not think it through.
