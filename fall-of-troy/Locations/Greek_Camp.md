# Greek Camp

## Basic Information
- **Type:** Camp
- **Status:** Standing
- **Controlled By:** [[Factions/Greeks]]
- **Notable Features:** The black ships beached along the shore, Achilles' tent at the end of the line, The assembly ground, The wall and ditch dug in Year 1

## Description & History
The Greek camp occupied the beach below Troy for ten years — a temporary city of tents, ships, and frustration stretched along the Trojan shoreline. The Greeks had pulled their ships onto the sand and built a wall and defensive ditch to protect them, acknowledging that Troy would not fall quickly.

Life in the camp was defined by the rhythms of siege: raids on surrounding towns, arguments among commanders, plague, supply shortages, and the daily cost of keeping a coalition of proud and independent kings pointed in roughly the same direction. Agamemnon commanded in name; in practice, authority shifted constantly between him, Achilles, Odysseus, and whoever had performed best in the most recent engagement.

The camp became a stage for the most consequential internal conflict of the war: Achilles' withdrawal after Agamemnon took Briseis from him. For weeks, Greeks died on the beach without their greatest fighter, the Trojans pressed to the ships themselves, and the tide of war turned against the besiegers. The damage done in those weeks — in lives and in the fractured trust between commanders — never fully healed.
