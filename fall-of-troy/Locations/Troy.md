# Troy

## Basic Information
- **Type:** City
- **Status:** Ruined
- **Controlled By:** [[Factions/Trojans]]
- **Notable Features:** The Scaean Gate, Walls built by Apollo and Poseidon, The citadel of Pergamon, The temple of Athena

## Description & History
Troy — also called Ilium — stood on a strategic promontory near the mouth of the Hellespont, commanding the sea routes between the Aegean and the Black Sea. For centuries it had profited from this position, accumulating wealth through trade and tribute. By the time of the war it was one of the great cities of the ancient world, with walls reputedly built by Apollo and Poseidon themselves during an earlier age of the gods.

The city endured ten years of siege without falling by force. Its walls were never breached by Greek arms. Troy fell not through military defeat but through a stratagem: a wooden horse left as an apparent offering, hollow and full of Greek soldiers, dragged inside the walls by the Trojans themselves. The city burned in a single night.

What remained was not quite nothing. Aeneas escaped with his father and the sacred Palladium, and the tradition he carried westward would eventually seed the city of Rome. But Troy itself — the towers, the temple, the Scaean Gate where Hector had kissed his wife and son for the last time — was ash.
