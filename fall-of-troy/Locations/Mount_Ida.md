# Mount Ida

## Basic Information
- **Type:** Mountain
- **Status:** Standing
- **Notable Features:** The peak where the Judgment of Paris took place, Favoured by Zeus as a vantage point, Sacred springs and forest groves

## Description & History
The great mountain southeast of Troy, Mount Ida was sacred to the gods and served as Zeus's preferred vantage point for watching the war unfold on the plain below. Its slopes were dense with forest and home to the springs of the river Scamander, which would later run red with the blood of Trojan and Greek dead.

It was on Mount Ida that Paris, while still a shepherd, was visited by Hermes bringing three goddesses — Hera, Athena, and Aphrodite — and the golden apple inscribed "for the fairest." Each goddess offered him a bribe. Hera offered power over kingdoms; Athena offered skill in war and wisdom; Aphrodite offered the most beautiful woman in the world. He chose Aphrodite. From that choice, Troy's fate was sealed.

The mountain appears again in the Iliad as a site of divine assembly, where Zeus watches battles and is sometimes persuaded — or tricked — into favouring one side over another. It represents the uncomfortable closeness of the divine world to the human one: the gods sitting above, choosing sides, placing bets on men's lives.
