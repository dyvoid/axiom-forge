## Description & History
The eldest son of King Priam and Queen Hecuba, Hector was Troy's greatest warrior and its most beloved defender. Where Achilles fought for personal glory, Hector fought for his city, his family, and the people who depended on him. This distinction — felt by every soldier who stood behind his shield — made him something rarer than a champion: a man worth dying for.

He led the Trojan forces through nine years of siege with discipline and intelligence, holding the walls against a coalition that vastly outnumbered Troy's defenders. He was not reckless; he was precise. He studied the Greeks, understood their strengths and weaknesses, and made careful choices that preserved Trojan lives across a decade of attrition.

When Patroclus entered battle wearing Achilles' armour, it was Hector who killed him — not knowing at the time whose armour he faced. This act made his own death inevitable. Achilles returned to battle consumed by grief and vengeance, and Hector — who had every reason to retreat behind the walls — chose to meet him in the open. He ran once, circling the city three times, but turned to face the end he had always known was coming. He asked only that his body be returned to his family. It was not, at first.
