# Priam

## Basic Information
- **Sex:** Male
- **Allegiance:** [[Factions/Trojans]]

## Description & History
King of Troy during the war, Priam was an old man by the time the Greeks beached their ships. He had ruled wisely for decades, built Troy into one of the wealthiest cities in the Aegean, and fathered fifty sons. The war found him too old to fight, so he watched it from the walls.

His most affecting moment in the Iliad comes after Hector's death, when he does what no one expects: he goes alone and unarmed to Achilles' tent, in the Greek camp, at night, to beg for his son's body. He kneels before the man who killed his son, kisses his hands, and appeals to him as one father to another — to think of Priam's own father, Peleus, who would never again hold his son either. Achilles wept. He returned the body. It was the most humane moment in the entire war.

Priam did not survive the fall of Troy. He was killed at the altar of Zeus by Neoptolemus, son of Achilles — the old king cut down in the place that should have offered sanctuary.
