## Description & History
The closest companion of Achilles — his childhood friend, battle-brother, and the person whose death finally broke Achilles' rage-fuelled withdrawal from the war. Patroclus was, by all accounts, the kinder of the two: the Greeks sought him out when they needed mercy or medicine; Achilles was approached when they needed someone killed.

When Achilles refused to fight after his quarrel with Agamemnon, it was Patroclus who could no longer bear watching the Greeks suffer. He begged Achilles to let him enter battle wearing his armour, so the Trojans would believe Achilles had returned. Achilles agreed, with strict instructions to drive the Trojans from the ships and no further. Patroclus, caught up in the momentum of success, pressed too close to the walls of Troy. Apollo struck him, staggered him, and Euphorbus wounded him. Hector delivered the killing blow.

His death is the pivot point of the Iliad. Everything before it leads up to it; everything after flows from it.
