# Diomedes

## Basic Information
- **Sex:** Male
- **Born:** c. 1242 BCE
- **Allegiance:** [[Factions/Greeks]]
- **Divine Patron:** [[Gods/Athena]]

## Description & History
The youngest of the Greek commanders and, by a considerable margin, the least troublesome. Diomedes quarrelled with no one, sulked in no tent, disputed no prize, and fought with a consistency that makes him easy to overlook next to louder men. Athena favoured him for exactly this reason: he took instruction.

In the war's fourth book she cleared the mist from his eyes so he could distinguish gods from men on the field, and he proceeded to wound two of them. He drove Aphrodite off with a spear through the wrist when she came to carry Aeneas out of danger, and later put Ares himself into retreat — the only mortal in the tradition to make a god of war leave a battlefield. He did it without arrogance, on instruction, and stopped when he was told to stop.

He and Odysseus made the most effective pairing in the Greek camp: one who could think of the plan and one who could execute it without improvising. They went over the walls together to steal the Palladium, which was the act that actually made Troy takeable. He went home afterwards to find his own house in disorder, and left again — but unlike almost everyone else in this war, he survived what he started.
