# Cassandra

## Basic Information
- **Sex:** Female
- **Born:** c. 1237 BCE
- **Died:** Shortly after the fall of Troy
- **Allegiance:** [[Factions/Trojans]]
- **Divine Patron:** [[Gods/Apollo]]

## Description & History
Apollo gave her prophecy in order to court her. She accepted the gift and then refused him, and since a god's gift cannot be withdrawn he amended it instead: she would see accurately and be believed by no one. It is the most precise curse in the tradition, because it leaves the victim entirely intact. Nothing is taken from her. She simply becomes unusable.

She was correct about everything. She named Paris as the ruin of the city while he was still a shepherd on [[Locations/Mount_Ida]]. She said the embassy to Sparta would end in fire. She stood in front of the wooden horse and told them exactly what was inside it, and the Trojans — who had ten years of evidence that she was never wrong — dragged it through the gate anyway. Her accuracy was never in question and never made any difference, which is a distinction worth holding onto: being right is not the same as being persuasive, and the tradition is clear that the second one is what saves cities.

She took sanctuary at Athena's altar during the sack and was dragged from it, an act of sacrilege so severe that the goddess who had spent ten years destroying Troy turned on the Greeks over it. She was given to Agamemnon, carried to Mycenae, and killed in his hall alongside him. She had described that ending too, on the way there, to people who did not believe her.
