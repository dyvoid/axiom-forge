# Menelaus

- **Spouse:** [[Humans/Helen]]

## Description & History
The younger brother of Agamemnon and king of Sparta by marriage rather than birth, Menelaus was the man the war was nominally fought for and the man least consulted about it. Helen was taken from his house; the oath sworn by her former suitors obliged a thousand ships; his brother took command of them. He spent ten years as the grievance rather than the general.

He was a competent fighter and a decent one, which in that company made him unremarkable. His single duel with Paris in the war's tenth year should have settled everything — he had beaten the man comprehensively and was dragging him from the field by the helmet strap when Aphrodite lifted her favourite out of the dust and set him down safely inside the walls. The war continued because a goddess found the ending unsatisfactory.

What distinguishes him is what he did at the end. Troy burned, and he went through it looking for Helen with a drawn sword and every intention of using it. He found her. He did not use it. They sailed home together and, by every account that follows, lived out a long and unremarkable marriage — the only figure in this story granted an ordinary ending.
