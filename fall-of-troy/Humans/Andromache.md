# Andromache

## Basic Information
- **Sex:** Female
- **Born:** c. 1238 BCE
- **Allegiance:** [[Factions/Trojans]]
- **Spouse:** [[Humans/Hector]]

## Description & History
Andromache had already lost one family before the war reached her second. Achilles sacked her father's city of Thebe in the campaign's early years, killed her father and seven brothers in a single day, and took her mother for ransom. She came to Troy with nothing left but a husband, and told him so plainly: *you are father and mother and brother to me now, as well as husband.*

Her scene at the Scaean Gate is the one moment in the Iliad where the war is argued about in domestic terms and the domestic case is allowed to be correct. She tells Hector where the walls are weakest and asks him to fight from the tower instead of the field — a sound tactical suggestion, offered by someone with no standing to offer it. He refuses, not because she is wrong but because he cannot be the man who does that. Their son cries at the sight of his father's helmet, and Hector takes it off to hold him. It is the last time.

She was in the palace when the city fell, and what happened afterwards is recorded with unusual specificity: allotted to Neoptolemus, son of the man who had destroyed her first family, and carried west as his property. Her son did not survive the sack. Of everyone who lived through the fall of Troy, she is the one the tradition refuses to let off.
