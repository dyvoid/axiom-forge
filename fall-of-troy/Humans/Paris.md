## Description & History
Second son of King Priam and Queen Hecuba, Paris was abandoned at birth after a prophecy declared he would be the ruin of Troy. He survived, was raised by shepherds on Mount Ida, and was eventually recognised and welcomed back into the royal family — the prophecy unbroken.

He was a skilled archer but no warrior in the Greek sense. When Menelaus challenged him to single combat to settle the war without further bloodshed, Paris nearly died and was saved only by Aphrodite spiriting him back to his chambers. Hector found this cowardice appalling. The soldiers who had spent years bleeding in the mud found it worse.

His role in starting the war is unambiguous: he was offered a choice on Mount Ida by three goddesses, he chose the bribe of the most beautiful woman in the world, and he acted on it with no apparent consideration of the consequences. In the final year of the war, guided by Apollo, it was his arrow that found Achilles' heel. He was himself killed shortly after by Philoctetes — fittingly, with the bow of Heracles.
