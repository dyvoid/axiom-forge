# Penelope

## Description & History
Penelope never went to Troy and is nonetheless one of the few people to win anything in this story. She held Ithaca for twenty years — ten of war and ten of her husband's absurdly extended return — against a house full of suitors who had eaten their way through the kingdom's herds and were waiting for her to concede that she was a widow.

Her method was her husband's, which is presumably why the marriage worked. She announced she would choose a new husband once she had finished weaving a shroud for Odysseus's father, wove it through each day, and unpicked it every night. It held for three years and was defeated only by a servant's betrayal. It is exactly the kind of solution Odysseus would have reached for: not a refusal, which would have been overruled, but a condition that could be indefinitely postponed.

When he finally came back, disguised and unannounced, she did not take his word for who he was. She had the marriage bed moved out of the chamber to receive him — knowing it could not be moved, because he had built it himself around a living olive tree still rooted in the floor. He objected before he could stop himself, and in objecting proved it. She had set a test he could only fail by passing. It is the one recognition scene in the tradition where the person being tested is the hero.
