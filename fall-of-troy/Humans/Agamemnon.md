# Agamemnon

## Basic Information
- **Epithets:** Lord of Men, King of Mycenae, Shepherd of the People
- **Sex:** Male
- **Born:** c. 1255 BCE
- **Died:** Shortly after his return from Troy
- **Allegiance:** [[Factions/Greeks]]

## Description & History
High King of Mycenae and commander of the Greek coalition, Agamemnon held the largest fleet and the weakest claim to the loyalty of the men who sailed in it. His authority rested not on excellence — he was neither the best fighter nor the best strategist in his own camp — but on the oath the suitors of Helen had sworn, and on the simple arithmetic of ships. Every king who followed him knew it.

That gap between rank and merit defined the campaign. When plague struck the Greek camp in the tenth year and the cause proved to be his refusal to return a priest's daughter, he yielded only to seize Achilles' prize instead — an act of pure precedence-keeping that cost the Greeks their greatest fighter for months and very nearly the war. He was capable of recognising his errors, and did so more than once at length and in public. He was rarely capable of preventing them.

He got his victory in the end, though it belonged more to Odysseus than to him. What he did not get was the return. Ten years of held-together coalition, of managing prouder men than himself, ended in his own hall at the hands of his wife Clytemnestra, who had spent those years attending to a grievance of her own.
