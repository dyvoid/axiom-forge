## Description & History
The Greek coalition — called Achaeans or Argives in the Iliad — was assembled by Agamemnon, High King of Mycenae, invoking the oath that bound all former suitors of Helen to defend her marriage. It was not a unified army but a confederation of independent kingdoms, each led by its own king, each with its own ships, soldiers, and ambitions.

The coalition included some of the greatest warriors of the age: Achilles with his Myrmidons, the two Ajaxes, Diomedes, Odysseus, Nestor the aged king of Pylos, and many others. Holding them together across ten years of failed siege tested every political skill Agamemnon possessed — and found many of those skills wanting. His quarrel with Achilles nearly destroyed the campaign from within.

The Greeks' ultimate victory owed as much to Odysseus's cunning as to any military achievement. Troy was never taken by force. It was taken by a story — the story of a horse.
